Bacha Hua Khana

Leftover food, reported by voice, on its way to a shelter tonight.

A kitchen says what's left in Hindi, Malayalam, Kannada or English. The app reads it back, calls a volunteer, and tells the shelter when it's coming.

How it works

  1. 1 The kitchen speaks

    Hold the button and say it: "50 plate biryani, 2 baje bana". The app reads it back, asks if it's veg and covered, and waits for a yes. It never acts on a guess.

  2. 2 A volunteer gets a call

    The nearest volunteer on duty who can carry it hears the offer in their own language and says yes or no. A no goes to the next one straight away.

  3. 3 The shelter receives

    A shelter asks for food by voice too. It sees who is coming and when, and says "yes, it's here" when the food arrives.

Open the app

  • Kitchen For a restaurant, mess or caterer with food left over: say what it is and how much.
  • Volunteer For someone with a scooter and an hour: hear an offer, say yes or no, follow the route.
  • Shelter For a shelter or NGO: ask for food for tonight and confirm when it arrives.
  • Dashboard For the coordinator: every rescue moving live, with each voice step's timing.

Built on Gnani AI

Prisma v2.5 speech to text

Turns what a kitchen, volunteer or shelter says into text, in their own language, including 8 kHz phone-line audio.

Timbre v2.5 text to speech

Speaks every read-back, safety question and volunteer offer, in the listener's language. Each phrase is cached, so a phrase is paid for once.

How well it understands

Checks that matched what was spoken, by recording condition

This is a prototype. Every kitchen, volunteer, shelter and phone number in it is made up, set around Kochi. The app asks the kitchen two safety questions (veg? covered and clean?), but in real use partner organisations would check the food before anyone eats it.
